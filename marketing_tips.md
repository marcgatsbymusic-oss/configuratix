# Mammut Marketing & CRO Analysis

This document outlines key strategies for elevating the Mammut product pages (e.g., IGLO EDGE) from technical spec sheets into high-converting sales engines.

## 1. Lead with Benefits, Not Just Specs (The "So What?" Factor)
While architects and builders care about technical specifications like "Uw = 0.66", a homeowner just sees numbers. 
* **The Fix:** Translate specs into tangible lifestyle benefits. Beneath or next to technical readouts, add micro-copy taglines.
  * Example: *"Uw = 0.66"* -> *"Unmatched insulation. Save up to 30% on your winter heating bills."*
  * Example: *"Sound insulation 47dB"* -> *"City noise stays outside. Whisper-quiet living spaces."*

## 2. Strengthen the Primary Hero CTA
The Hero section is the most expensive real estate on the page. While floating action bars at the bottom of the screen are great, the Hero section must capture high-intent users immediately.
* **The Fix:** Add two prominent buttons to the Hero banner right below the main tagline:
  * **Primary CTA:** `[ CONFIGURE & PRICE ]` (High intent, drives them directly to the 3D visualizer/configurator tool).
  * **Secondary CTA:** `[ DOWNLOAD BROCHURE ]` or `[ VIEW GALLERY ]` (Low intent, keeps top-of-funnel users engaged).

## 3. Inject "Lifestyle" Context & Emotional Storytelling
Technical pages often rely heavily on isolated 3D renders, profile cross-sections, and technical blueprints. While this feels premium, it lacks human connection. Windows are how people view the world from their living rooms.
* **The Fix:** Introduce an **"Inspirations"** or **"Gallery"** section showing the products installed in stunning, real-world modern homes. Show sunlight streaming through the glass. People buy the *result* (a beautiful home), not the *tool* (the window frame).

## 4. Build Trust with Social Proof & Authority
Premium products need premium trust signals. Users need immediate visual confirmation that they are making a safe, high-end investment.
* **The Fix:** Add a dedicated **"Why Choose Mammut?"** trust band immediately after the Hero section.
  * Include warranty badges (e.g., *"10-Year Comprehensive Warranty"*).
  * Include certification logos (e.g., *Passivhaus Certified, CE marked*).
  * Feature a carousel of 2-3 short, punchy customer or architect testimonials.

## 5. Create a "Sticky" Table of Contents for Long Scrolling
Highly detailed "megapages" (Colors, Hardware, Glass, Specs, Downloads) can cause users to get lost or fatigued while scrolling.
* **The Fix:** Implement a sleek, horizontal sticky sub-navigation menu just under the header that locks to the top of the screen on scroll. Links like `[Overview]`, `[Colors]`, `[Hardware]`, `[Glass]`, `[Specs]` allow users to jump instantly to what they care about without endless scrolling.

## 6. Strategic Friction: Gated Lead Generation
Offering direct PDF links for catalogues is great for UX, but it is a missed opportunity for B2B marketing and lead capture.
* **The Fix:** If you want to generate B2B leads (contractors, architects) or high-ticket B2C leads, consider "gating" the highly technical catalogue. Make the high-level Brochure free to download, but for the full technical Catalogue, use a sleek pop-up: *"Enter your email to download the full architectural spec sheet."* This provides the sales team with a warm lead to follow up on.
